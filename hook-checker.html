Is your opening line doing its job?

LinkedIn cuts a post short after roughly the first couple of lines, behind a "see more" — and a Twitter/X thread's first tweet is the only thing most people ever see before deciding whether to open the rest. This pulls out just that first line and checks it for the two most common ways it quietly fails: a tired opener that a reader has seen a hundred times, or a line so long it never gets to the point before it's cut off.

The "first couple of lines" truncation point is well-documented LinkedIn UI behavior, not an exact fixed number — it shifts a little by device and font size, so treat length guidance here as a rule of thumb. See, e.g., this 2026 LinkedIn algorithm breakdown and Ship 30 for 30's thread-hook guide for the same advice applied to X/Twitter threads.

This only judges the opening line mechanically — its length and whether it matches a list of common cliché openers. It can't tell you if your hook is actually interesting; that part's still on you. Thread numbering like "1/" is stripped automatically before checking.

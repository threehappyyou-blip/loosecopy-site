How hard is this to read?

Flesch-Kincaid has measured reading difficulty since the 1970s, using two things only: how long your sentences run and how many syllables your words carry. It won't tell you if your writing is good — but it gives you an objective number for whether a reader has to work harder than they should to follow you, which matters even more when a chunk of your audience isn't reading in their first language.

Formula and interpretation bands: Wikipedia — Flesch–Kincaid readability tests.

Syllable counts are estimated with a standard vowel-pattern heuristic, not a pronunciation dictionary — it lands close on most words (tested around 80-85% exact-match on common English words) but will occasionally miscount an individual word. Averaged across a whole paragraph, that noise mostly cancels out, so treat the score as a solid directional read rather than a lab-precise measurement. Word/sentence splitting is also approximate — for example, "3.5" or an ellipsis can occasionally throw off a count by one.

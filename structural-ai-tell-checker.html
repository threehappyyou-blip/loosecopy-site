Does your writing have a rhythm, or a pattern?

Three structural habits that show up a lot in AI-generated text: sentences that are all nearly the same length, heavy em dash use, and the "it's not just X — it's Y" construction repeated more than once. None of these prove anything by themselves — plenty of human writing does all three — but more than one showing up together is worth a second look.

Uniform sentence length ("low burstiness") and heavy em dash use are both widely discussed as patterns that show up more in AI output than in typical human writing — see, e.g., this explainer on burstiness and perplexity and this piece on the em dash as an AI "tell". These are informal, widely-repeated observations, not peer-reviewed findings — treat the flag here the same way.

This only looks at sentence-length statistics, em dash characters, and one sentence template — nothing here reads for meaning, and nothing here calls an AI model. A technical write-up, a list of short instructions, or just a naturally consistent writer can also score "uniform" — it's a prompt to skim, not an accusation.

Does this sound too good to be true?

Finds unhedged, absolute-certainty phrases — "100% guaranteed," "never fails," "everyone agrees," "scientifically proven" — in your draft. A claim with no room for exception is the easiest kind for a skeptical reader to pick apart, and it's also one of the most common tells of marketing-speak or AI-generated hype copy. This doesn't check whether the claim is true — only whether you said it in a way that invites a "really? always?" reply.

Flagged: "100% guaranteed," "never fails," "everyone agrees," "without a doubt"  ·  Not flagged: "usually works," "in our testing," "most users," "100% of the 40 people surveyed" (a real stat, not a certainty word)
If the claim involves a number, the unsourced-statistic checker is the sibling tool — this one catches overconfident wording, that one catches missing sources. Back to the generator →

This only matches a curated list of absolute-certainty phrases (not common words like "all" or "always" on their own, which show up constantly in ordinary writing) — so it stays quiet unless the wording is genuinely unhedged. It can still miss an overconfident claim phrased in an unusual way, and a flag doesn't mean the claim is false — just that it's worded in a way that's easy to challenge.

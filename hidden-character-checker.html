Is anything invisible hiding in your post?

Text copied out of an AI chat, a document, or a web page — or typed with a Chinese, Japanese or Korean keyboard — can carry characters you can't see: zero-width spaces, spaces that only look normal, full-width punctuation like ， or ！, a space before a question mark. They can split a hashtag or @mention in two, make search miss your post, or show up as odd gaps. This finds them and hands you a cleaned copy.

Copying from an AI chat, a doc, or a web page picks these up far more often than typing straight into the post box. If you also write in Chinese, Japanese or Korean, switch your keyboard to half-width mode before typing English. Run all 21 checks on this text →

About the "hidden AI watermark" rumor: narrow no-break spaces (U+202F) really have turned up in AI chat output — users reported GPT-5 doing it in October 2025, where it showed up as squashed gaps on some apps. There's no evidence it's a watermark, and cleaning it out won't make AI-written text pass a detector — detectors score the writing itself. It's still worth cleaning for how the post displays.

Rule-based, not a parser — it looks for a fixed list of invisible and look-alike characters, and it leaves full-width punctuation alone when it sits next to Chinese, Japanese or Korean text, where it belongs. Emoji that are built from several pieces (like 👨‍👩‍👧) are left intact. It doesn't check spelling or grammar.

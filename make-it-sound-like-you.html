Make it sound like you, specifically

The review checkbox says "I've made it mine." Here's what that actually looks like in practice — six concrete edits, not "just read it over."

1. Cut the windup sentence

AI drafts almost always throat-clear before getting to the point. Delete the first sentence and see if the paragraph still works — it usually does, and it's usually better.

BeforeIn today's fast-paced digital landscape, it's more important than ever to stay connected with your audience.
AfterI posted twice a week for a year. Then I missed a month, and engagement didn't even dip.

2. Swap one generic line for one real detail

Genericness is the biggest tell there is — not word choice, genericness. One specific, only-you-would-know detail (a number, a name, a place, a specific failure) does more than rewriting the whole paragraph.

BeforeMany people struggle with productivity when working from home.
AfterI got nothing done my first two weeks of WFH because my desk faced a wall I'd started painting and never finished.

3. Break the rhythm

AI writing tends toward evenly-sized sentences, one after another. Real voices are lumpy — a long sentence, then a short one, sometimes a fragment. If every sentence in the draft is roughly the same length, that alone reads as machine-smoothed, independent of the words.

BeforeThe process was challenging. It required patience and dedication. Ultimately, the results were rewarding.
AfterIt was a slog, honestly. Took way longer than I planned. Worth it though.

4. Cut the hedge, keep the opinion

AI drafts hedge reflexively — "it's worth noting," "may not work for everyone, but worth considering." A real opinion just says the thing. If you actually believe it, say it without the safety net.

BeforeIt's worth noting that this approach may not work for everyone, but it's certainly worth considering.
AfterThis won't work for everyone. It worked for me.

5. Put back one of your actual habits

You probably have a verbal tic — a phrase you overuse, a habit of starting sentences with "and," a specific way you emphasize things. AI drafts smooth all of that away because it reads as "inconsistent." Putting one back in is disproportionately effective, because it's the kind of detail that's hard to fake and easy to recognize.

6. Don't just swap synonyms

Running a thesaurus over the draft doesn't change the voice — the sentence structure and rhythm are what actually read as "written by AI," not individual word choices. If you're only swapping words and the sentence shape underneath is untouched, you haven't actually changed much. Rewrite the sentence, not just the words in it.

The 10-minute pass

  1. Read it out loud once. Anywhere you stumble or wouldn't actually say it that way — rewrite it.
  2. Delete the first sentence of each paragraph. Add it back only if the paragraph genuinely needs it.
  3. Find one place to add a real, specific detail only you would know.
  4. Find one hedge word or phrase ("it's worth noting," "arguably," "in many cases") and either cut it or commit to the opinion underneath it.

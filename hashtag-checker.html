Are your hashtags doing anything for you?

AI drafts either skip hashtags entirely or dump ten of them at the end out of habit. Neither is great: zero hashtags on LinkedIn is a missed discovery opportunity, and a wall of them reads as keyword-stuffing. This counts what you've got, flags accidental duplicates, and points out tags so long and glued-together they're hard to read.

The 3–5 range referenced here is the commonly repeated LinkedIn guidance for 2026 (see, e.g., this breakdown) — treat it as a rule of thumb, not a hard cutoff; the underlying studies behind it aren't independently published. The camelCase suggestion for long multi-word tags follows standard social-media accessibility guidance, e.g. Accessible Social's hashtag formatting guide — screen readers can misread an all-lowercase glued tag as one garbled word.

Only tags starting with a letter are counted, on purpose — things like "#1" or "#2026" are usually just numbers in a sentence, not real hashtags, so counting them would overstate things. X/Twitter's own hashtag guidance is thinner and less settled than LinkedIn's, so the count-range advice here leans LinkedIn-specific.

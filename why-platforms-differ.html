Same idea, two different jobs

When Loosecopy hands you back a Twitter thread and a LinkedIn post from the same notes, they shouldn't read like the same text split in two. Here's the actual, sourced case for why — and what to check for when you review each one.

Last verified: September 2026

X (Twitter)

Sweet spot
71–100characters per post for peak engagement
What that buys you
Posts under 100 characters see roughly 17% higher engagement than longer ones
Tone
Punchy, conversational — one idea per post, threaded for the rest
Hashtags
Short if used at all — long tags read as noise here

LinkedIn

Sweet spot
~150characters before "see more" truncates it
What that buys you
Long-form posts (1,900–2,000 words) perform best when the topic supports it
Tone
Professional, substantive — room to actually explain your thinking
What matters most
The first ~150 characters have to work alone — most readers never click "see more"

These aren't arbitrary style preferences — they come from how each platform actually gets consumed. X is a fast-scrolling feed where brevity is rewarded algorithmically and socially. LinkedIn folds anything past a few lines behind a click, so the opening has to stand alone, but readers who do expand are looking for something substantive, not a tweet in a blazer.

So when you review your two drafts

  • On the Twitter thread: does the first tweet work completely on its own? That's the only one most people will ever see — if it needs the second tweet to make sense, tighten it.
  • On the LinkedIn post: read only the first ~150 characters, then stop. If that alone doesn't make you want to click "see more," the rest of the post won't save it.
  • Across both: if the LinkedIn draft reads like a thread with the line breaks removed, or the Twitter draft reads like a shrunk-down essay, that's the sign to ask for — or make — real edits, not just a trim.

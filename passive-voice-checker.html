How much of this is passive voice?

"The feature was launched" reads differently than "we launched the feature" — the first hides who actually did it. AI drafts lean passive by habit, and it's a hard thing to catch in your own writing since it doesn't sound wrong, just softer. This looks for be-verb + participle patterns ("was written," "got shared," "has been reviewed") that usually mean passive voice, so you can decide where naming the "who" would land better.

Same detection approach (be-verb followed by a past participle, with a "by ..." phrase as a confidence signal) used by established tools like AnalyzeMyWriting's passive voice detector and the pattern Grammarly/Hemingway-style checkers are built on.

This is pattern-matching, not real grammar parsing — it can't reliably tell true passive voice ("was designed by the team") apart from a participial adjective ("was excited," "was tired"), so treat every highlight as a candidate to glance at, not a verdict. It also misses some real passives (contractions like "it's written" aren't caught) and passive voice isn't always wrong — sometimes the actor genuinely doesn't matter ("the site was down for an hour"). Use judgment, not a zero target.

How the passive voice checker works

It looks for a form of “to be” (is, are, was, were, been, being) followed by a past participle, like “was launched” or “has been reviewed,” and for informal passives with “get,” like “got shared.” When a “by …” phrase follows, as in “was reviewed by the team,” the match is marked as more certain, because the sentence names the doer after the fact.

The rules are fixed and there’s no AI involved, so the same text always gets the same result, and every highlight shows which words triggered it.

When passive voice is the better choice

Passive voice isn’t a grammar error. It often reads better when the doer is unknown or beside the point (“The office was broken into overnight”), when the result matters more than who caused it (“Shipping fees were cut by half”), and in research write-ups, where passive is the norm.

It causes trouble when it hides your own part in something. “Mistakes were made” or “The update was pushed early” leave readers asking who did it. Those are the sentences worth rewriting with a clear subject.

Examples

BeforeThe new pricing was announced on Monday, and the feedback was reviewed by the team.
AfterWe announced the new pricing on Monday, and the team reviewed the feedback.
BeforeMistakes were made during the rollout.
AfterWe made mistakes during the rollout. Here’s what we changed.

Questions people ask

What is passive voice?

A sentence is passive when the thing being acted on becomes the subject: “The report was written by Sam” instead of “Sam wrote the report.” The person doing the action moves into a “by” phrase or drops out of the sentence.

Is passive voice wrong?

No. It’s a choice. It reads as vague when it hides who did something, and natural when the doer doesn’t matter. The checker shows where it appears so you can decide sentence by sentence.

Does it catch every passive sentence?

No. It catches a be-verb plus a participle (“was launched”) and informal “get” passives (“got approved”). It doesn’t yet catch passives after “will be,” “can be” or “should be,” and it can mistake an adjective for a participle, as in “I was excited.”

Will it rewrite my sentences for me?

No. It highlights each passive construction and says why it was flagged. You choose whether to change it, so the post stays in your own words.

Is my text sent anywhere?

No. There’s no sign-up, the check runs in your browser, and nothing you paste is stored or sent.

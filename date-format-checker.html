Which date did you mean?

Paste your draft and find any numeric date — like 3/4/2026 — that genuinely reads two different ways depending on where your reader lives. Americans read month-first (March 4); most of the rest of the world reads day-first (April 3). This only flags dates where both readings are actually possible — it leaves ISO dates ("2026-03-04") and spelled-out dates ("March 4, 2026") alone, since those already read the same way everywhere.

Ambiguous: 3/4/2026, 03-04-26, 9.10.2026  ·  Not ambiguous: 2026-03-04 (ISO), 25/12/2026 (25 can only be a day), March 4 2026

This checks the format only, not whether the date itself is correct. A date is flagged as ambiguous only when both the first and second number could plausibly be a month (1–12) and they differ — "25/12/2026" isn't flagged because 25 can only be a day. If you genuinely mean the same date either way (like 3/3/2026), it's left alone too.

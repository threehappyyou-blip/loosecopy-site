The draft is only as good as what you paste in

Most of the editing work people do after generating a draft could've been avoided with about five minutes of prep before hitting Generate. This is that five minutes.

"Make it sound like you" is about fixing a draft after it comes back generic. This page is the earlier half of that problem — what you give the AI in the first place has more influence on how generic the result sounds than any edit you'll make afterward.

Five things worth doing before you paste

Paste it rough, not cleaned up

If you tidy your notes into full, proper sentences before pasting, you've already erased the one thing that would've made the draft sound like you: your actual phrasing. Fragments and messy asides are fine input — better than fine, actually.

Keep one real, specific detail

A number you actually remember, a name, an exact moment. Vague input produces vague output — there's nothing for the model to be specific about if you weren't.

vague: "I learned a lot from this project." specific: "I broke the staging deploy at 2am and learned to check the env file first, every time, no exceptions."

Say your point out loud once, then type that sentence

If you can say the actual point in one plain spoken sentence, put that literal sentence in your notes. It tends to survive into the draft closer to word-for-word than anything else you give it — there's simply nowhere else for the model to invent from.

Leave your opinion in, even if it's blunt

If you already think "this was a mistake" or "everyone gets this part wrong," write that down as-is. Don't sand it into neutral summary before pasting — the model will happily flatten it further on its own. You don't need to do that work for it in advance.

Mark the 2–3 sentences that actually matter

If your source is long, note which parts you'd keep if you had to cut it in half. Those are the parts most likely to get lost in a generic summary otherwise — and the parts most worth protecting into both drafts.

Before you hit Generate

  • At least one specific detail — a name, number, or exact moment — is in there
  • I didn't rewrite my notes into "proper" sentences first
  • If I have an opinion on this, it's written down, not just implied
